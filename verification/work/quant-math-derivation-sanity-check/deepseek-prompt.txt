# Quant math derivation sanity check — `quant-math-derivation-sanity-check`

**Deliverable type:** CHECK. This atom sells the check it produces — a bounded judgment artifact — never the underlying knowledge itself.

## What it delivers

Bound capability (from the skill): the rigorous math layer — linear algebra, probability, and statistics derived and sanity-checked, not just library calls. Its Practice line is this atom's method: derive before you trust; check dimensions and units; verify with a small numerical example; know each method's assumptions and failure modes; prefer the simplest method that fits.

A caller submits the inputs the bound skill works on and receives one check back, in the contract's output shape (see `contract.yaml`). One unit of sale = one delivered result.

## Buyer

First buyer hypothesis (from register data; not yet market-tested): teams and agents working in Thinking & decisions who need this check on demand and would rather buy it per use than build or staff the capability themselves.

## Self-use (R3 — passed)

Our own workflow that calls it: **Derivations for RA and EE analytics**. Wright is an internal caller on this atom (zero price, metered to the internal cost ledger — see `rails/billing.md`).

## Inputs — the derivation manifest

derivation record for one derivation, made from the derivation
as actually written. Every field below is read by the named
section's checks. A field **absent** = evidence missing
(`INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`); a field present but false / invalid =
the check fails (`DERIVATION_NOT_SOUND`). All fields are
booleans unless noted.

Nine checks are computed from the declared rows, using only
the skill's own stated practice plus the consistency of the
declared record itself — no outside threshold is imported:

- **Steps list declared:** the declared `steps` list passes
  when it is present and names at least one step. An absent
  list is missing evidence; an empty list fails — it declares
  no derivation at all, and the skill's first practice rule is
  to derive before trusting.
- **Step ids unique:** no declared `step_id` may appear on
  more than one row. A row with `step_id` absent is missing
  evidence for this check.
- **Operations recognised:** every declared step's `operation`
  is one of `add`, `subtract`, `multiply`, `divide` — the four
  operations whose arithmetic and unit algebra this record
  format defines. A row with `operation` absent is missing
  evidence; any other value fails.
- **Step arithmetic recomputes:** every declared step's
  `declared_result` must recompute from its declared operands
  under its declared operation, compared on the fixed 6dp
  serialization below. A row missing its operation or any of
  its three values is missing evidence; a row whose recomputed
  value differs fails, and is named in
  `derivation_steps_with_arithmetic_mismatch`.
- **No step divides by zero:** no declared `divide` step has a
  zero `operand_b`. A row missing its operation or `operand_b`
  is missing evidence for this check.
- **Every step declares all units:** every declared step
  declares `operand_a_unit`, `operand_b_unit` and
  `declared_result_unit`, none empty. A row with a unit field
  absent is missing evidence; a row declaring an empty unit
  fails.
- **Result units follow the unit algebra:** every declared
  step's `declared_result_unit` must be the unit the record's
  declared unit algebra produces from its operand units — for
  `add` / `subtract` the operands must share one unit and the
  result keeps it; for `multiply` the result unit is
  `<a_unit>*<b_unit>`; for `divide` it is `<a_unit>/<b_unit>`;
  the dimensionless unit `"1"` drops out of a product, a
  quotient by `"1"` keeps the numerator unit, and a quotient of
  a unit by itself is `"1"`. A row whose units are incomplete
  is missing evidence for this check; a row whose result unit
  differs fails, and is named in
  `derivation_steps_with_unit_mismatch`.
- **Final result value matches the last step:** the declared
  `derivation.final_result_value` must equal the last declared
  step's `declared_result`, compared on the 6dp serialization.
  Missing steps, a missing final value, or a last step without
  a declared result is missing evidence.
- **Final result unit matches the last step:** the declared
  `derivation.final_result_unit` must equal the last declared
  step's `declared_result_unit`. Either side absent or empty
  is missing evidence.

The output also reports the computed `computed_status`
(`within_stated_limits_on_declared_evidence` /
`outside_stated_limits_on_declared_evidence` / null when any of
the nine computed checks above cannot be completed),
`derivation_steps_count` (the declared list length, or null
when the list is not declared — counts are reported as
integers, exactly), `duplicate_derivation_step_ids` (ids
declared on more than one row, in the order their second
occurrence appears), the row-name lists
`derivation_steps_with_arithmetic_mismatch` and
`derivation_steps_with_unit_mismatch` (steps complete enough
to verify whose declared values do not verify; a step with a
field absent is missing evidence and does not appear),
`derivation_steps_missing_units` (steps with any unit field
absent or empty), and the ratios `arithmetic_match_ratio`
(steps whose arithmetic recomputes ÷ declared steps) and
`unit_match_ratio` (steps whose result unit follows the unit
algebra ÷ declared steps). **Fixed serialization rule:**
computed ratios are reported as strings with exactly six
decimal places, produced in both implementations by the same
rule — round half up to 6dp, `floor(v * 1000000 + 0.5) /
1000000`, then format with exactly six decimals; arithmetic
and final-result comparisons are made on the same 6dp
serializations of the recomputed and declared values. A ratio
is null when its denominator list is absent or empty. That
fixed serialization is what makes the Python and TypeScript
outputs byte-identical.

The skill states no numeric tolerance and no numeric threshold
at all — its demands are exact recomputation, declared
assumption checks and qualitative declarations the record
makes, not bars the atom can set. Quantities are computed and
reported; the only bars on the computed rows are exact
recomputation and completeness over the caller's own declared
steps, exactly as stated above. Declined thresholds, named: an
arithmetic tolerance or epsilon beyond the fixed 6dp
serialization (the skill states none, so a declared step that
differs at the 6dp serialization fails rather than passing
approximately); a p-value or significance level (no hypothesis
test is run or graded); a confidence level or interval width;
a correlation, covariance or variance magnitude bar; a minimum
or maximum number of derivation steps beyond at least one; a
convergence epsilon, iteration count or gradient norm for
optimization derivations; a probability-mass or distribution
fit bar (normality and the other assumptions are checked as
declared where-assumed attestations, never re-tested from
data — the atom receives no data); a numerical-example size or
closeness bar beyond the declaration that a small example was
verified; any judgment that the chosen method is the
mathematically right one for the problem (only that a method
is named, is declared the simplest that fits, and is declared
justified from the problem's structure is checked).

The verdict is one of `DERIVATION_SOUND` (all 30 checks pass),
`DERIVATION_NOT_SOUND` (any check fails), or
`INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` (nothing fails but evidence is
missing), across six sections: derivation_structure,
step_arithmetic, dimensions_and_units,
assumptions_and_failure_modes, method_choice_and_verification,
red_lines.

## Honest scoping

This atom reviews a **declared** derivation record — it derives
nothing itself, solves no problem, runs no model, test or
statistical procedure, inspects no data, code or paper, and
does not verify that any declared check (of dimensions, units,
assumptions or a numerical example) was ever actually carried
out. A derivation is sound *on the caller's declared evidence*:
a record that declares its assumptions checked, its failure
modes named and every step recomputing passes those checks
even if the real derivation did none of it. It is a
completeness and consistency audit of the derivation record
the skill requires, **not a proof checker, a mathematics
review, or a guarantee that the derivation is correct**: it
never judges whether a declared step is a valid mathematical
inference (only that its declared arithmetic recomputes and
its declared units follow the algebra), whether the declared
assumptions actually hold, whether the named method is
appropriate, or whether the final result answers the stated
goal — only that the record contains what the skill's practice
and anti-patterns demand, and that its declared steps are
internally consistent.

Named exclusions and non-reducible parts (carried as caller
declarations only, never judged): symbolic or multi-operand
steps — the record format covers exactly the four binary
operations above, and a derivation step that is a symbolic
manipulation, a limit, an integral, a matrix operation or any
other transformation is outside this atom's computed reach
(it can be declared in the step record only insofar as it
reduces to a declared binary arithmetic step; the atom takes
no position on such steps' validity); no numeric thresholds
at all (the skill states none — see the declined list above);
no live verification of any declared fact; no judgment of
derivation *quality* — whether the mathematics is right is the
caller's work, checked only for declared presence and internal
consistency; the skill's topic catalogue (linear algebra,
probability, statistics, calculus and optimization, stochastic
and quant methods) is not itself checked topic by topic — the
atom checks the practice the skill wraps around every topic,
not coverage of any topic.
